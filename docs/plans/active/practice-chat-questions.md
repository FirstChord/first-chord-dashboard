# Practice Chat: The Three Questions and How They Are Shown

Proposal, not yet built. Finn's decision is needed on the wording and on the
screen refresh before anything ships. Related baseline:
[Practice Note Quality](./practice-note-quality.md).

## Why the questions matter more than they look

Every tutor ends every lesson by answering these three prompts aloud, usually
with the student beside them reading the same screen. So the wording does not
only shape the note; it shapes what happens in the last minutes of the lesson,
across every tutor at once. A wording change is a school-wide teaching change
made with one deploy, and should be treated as an experiment: measure before,
change one thing, measure after.

The three questions already follow the best-supported model of self-regulated
learning (Zimmerman, applied to music by McPherson): **look back** (what
happened), **evaluate** (how it went and why), **plan** (what next). That
structure is right and should stay. The work is in the wording and the screen.

## What the research says, applied

| Finding | Source | Consequence for the questions |
|---|---|---|
| Recalling something is a stronger learning event than being told it again (retrieval practice), and the effect holds for primary-age children across ability levels. | [Karpicke, Blunt and Smith 2016](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4786565/) | Q1 is a learning moment, not just admin, **if the student recalls first**. |
| Open recall invitations ("tell me about", "what else?") get the fullest answers from children; yes/no and vague "how was it" questions get one word. | [Children's perceptions of open vs closed questions](https://www.nationalcac.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/%e2%80%9cShe-Wanted-to-Know-the-Full-Story%e2%80%9d-Childrens-perceptions-of-open-versus-closed-questions.pdf); [best-practice consensus](https://insights.taylorenglish.com/post/102hf6e/open-ended-questions-elicit-more-information-from-children) | Ask concrete "what" questions. Avoid "How did it go?" as the spoken question. |
| Two questions joined into one get one half answered. | [Kantar on double-barrelled questions](https://www.kantar.com/inspiration/research-services/double-barrelled-questions-pf) | Today's Q2 ("What went well **or** what was challenging?") invites either-or. Show two short questions instead. |
| "What" questions build insight; "why" questions about yourself tend towards rumination. | [Eurich, via TED Ideas](https://ideas.ted.com/the-right-way-to-be-introspective-yes-theres-a-wrong-way) | No "why" in student-facing prompts. "What helped?" not "Why did it work?" |
| Students who put setbacks down to **strategy or effort** respond more constructively than those who put them down to ability. | [Attribution research in music](https://ojs.ehu.eus/index.php/psicodidactica/article/download/6942/8175/33795) | Add "What helped?" as a prompt: it steers the student towards strategy, which they control. "Still tricky" also implies progress, not a fixed limit. |
| A plan that names when and where is followed through far more often than a goal alone (94 studies, d≈0.65); the if-then form ("when X, I'll Y") carries much of the effect. | [Gollwitzer and Sheeran](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/implementation-intentions) | Q3 should produce a plan with a time and place, not just a list of pieces. |
| Habits form through repetition in a **stable context**: same time, same place, same preceding cue. | [Lally et al. 2010; Wood and Neal](https://www.thebehavioralscientist.com/glossary/habit-formation) | "Where" earns its place: the same spot, with the instrument out, is the cue. |
| Students practise a piece they chose with markedly more deliberate strategies. Autonomy-supportive teachers ask for the student's view and act on it. | [Renwick and McPherson 2002](https://bulletproofmusician.com/what-changes-when-a-student-selects-their-own-repertoire-a-case-study/); [Reeve](https://www.acu.edu.au/about-acu/faculties-directorates-and-staff/faculty-of-education-and-arts/faculty-research/research-translations/hope-collection/how-can-teachers-effectively-learn-to-become-more-autonomy-supportive-to-enhance-student-motivation-and-wellbeing) | Q3 belongs to the student: "your plan". |
| Spacing practice out helps verbal memory strongly, but the evidence for complex motor skills like piano is mixed. | [Simmons, lack of spacing effects in piano](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5553926/) | Do **not** build "short and often" into the question. Leave "how much" to the tutor. |

## The wording problem with today's Q3

"What will you practise this week, and how, when and where?" asks four things
in one breath. It is accurate but reads as a form, and spoken aloud it invites
the student to answer the first part and stop. The fix is to **separate the
question you say from the checklist you glance at**: one clean spoken question,
with the four parts shown underneath as small prompts.

## Proposed wording

Revised 2026-10-08 after Finn's review: prompts as small "chips" read like
tabs and "Last week's plan" / "When" / "Where" did not make sense on their
own, so each question has at most **one gentle follow-up line** in lighter
text, phrased the way a tutor would say it.

| Step | Today | Proposed question | Follow-up line (lighter) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What did we do in the lesson? | **What did we work on today?** | none |
| 2 | What went well or what was challenging? | **What went well? / What's still tricky?** (two lines) | And what helped? |
| 3 | What will you practise this week, and how, when and where? | **What's your plan for this week?** | What to play, how to practise it, and a time and place to do it. |

Why each choice:

- **Q1** "work on" draws out pieces and techniques rather than a list of
  activities, and "today" is shorter and warmer than "in the lesson". Tutors
  can let the student try to recall it first, which turns the summary into
  retrieval practice.
- **Q2** becomes two single questions, so both get answered. "Still tricky"
  is child-friendly and carries a not-yet message. "And what helped?" steers
  the student to strategy. (Closing the loop on last week's plan is still
  worth doing, but as the actual text of last week's plan, not an abstract
  prompt. Not in this change.)
- **Q3** "your plan" makes it the student's and is the implementation-intention
  word. The follow-up line carries what, how, when and where in plain
  speech: "a time and place" is when and where without naming them.
  Tutors can model the if-then form out loud: "So when you get home on
  Tuesday, you'll…"

Options considered and not chosen:

- **Q2 "How did it go?"**: natural, but children answer it with "good".
- **Q2 "What went well, and what's still tricky?"**: one question with an
  "and" is still double-barrelled; two lines reads more cleanly.
- **Q3 "What will you practise, and when?"**: better than today but still two
  questions, and it drops "how", the part students most often lack.

The note's section labels (`[What we did]`, `[Progress & Challenges]`,
`[Practice Goals]`) are unchanged by any of this. They are a parsing contract
with the dashboard (`lib/admin/practice-notes-helpers.mjs`), so renaming
"Practice Goals" to "Practice plan" would be a separate, deliberate change.

## The screen

Practice Chat runs inside the dashboard's slide-over panel (768px wide), and
that panel already shows "Practice Chat" and the student's name. Measured
against the current stylesheet:

1. **The question is the smallest heading on the screen** (18px, weight 500)
   while "Question 1 of 3" is 20px/600 and "Record Your Answer" 18px/600.
2. **Progress is shown three times**: the 1-2-3 dots, "Question 1 of 3" and a
   "1/3" badge.
3. **The logo header repeats the panel header** and uses about a fifth of the
   height, inside the panel.
4. **The recording banner pushes everything down about 60px**, so the Stop
   button moves under the cursor.
5. **Nothing shows the microphone is hearing anything** while recording.

Proposed, as a subtle refresh in the same colours and typeface:

- **Named steps** instead of numbers: The lesson · How it went · The plan.
  They show the shape of the ritual and match the note's three sections.
- **Keep the illustration and wordmark** as a smaller welcome above the steps
  (about 184px wide, no tagline). Finn: the panel "has to feel like a nice
  place to be" when it opens.
- **One card** per step: the question as the largest text (about 30px, bold),
  the follow-up line in lighter grey, then the record button.
- **Recording state inside the button, kept calm**: a softly breathing dot,
  "Listening…" and "Tap to stop". No level meter and no timer: the student
  watches this screen, and a moving meter or a running clock is pressure.
  The microphone only speaks up when something is wrong: after a few seconds
  of silence, "Can't hear anything. Check the microphone." appears beneath.
  No banner, so nothing moves.
- Back / Type notes instead / Skip stay as quiet links below the card.

The mock used for the comparison images is a standalone page with no app
logic; it is a picture of the proposal, not an implementation.

## Measuring it

Baseline from 271 notes (2026-09-23 to 2026-10-07), measured on the Practice
Goals section by keyword markers: names a strategy **14%**, an amount **7%**,
a time **2%**; student voice present in **3%** of notes. Note: the Q3 wording
"how, when and where" went live 2026-10-07, so a re-measure in late October
mixes that change with any later one. Change one thing at a time, and re-run
the same markers about three weeks after each change.

## Build outline (when approved)

1. `QUESTIONS` in practice-chat `public/src/app.js` becomes objects with
   `lines` and `prompts`, rendered by `updateQuestionDisplay()`.
2. Question card markup and styles in `index.html` / `css/styles.css`; remove
   the duplicate progress elements.
3. Recording state moves into `mainActionBtn` ("Listening…", breathing dot);
   the `status` banner is no longer used while recording. An `AnalyserNode` on
   the live `MediaStream` is used only to detect sustained silence, never shown
   as a meter.
4. Header shrinks to the illustration and wordmark, without the tagline.
5. Re-record the demo GIF (`npm run demo:record`), whose captions mention the
   questions.
